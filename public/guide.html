Riff Catalog User Guide

How every part of the app works, with pictures. No music-theory degree required.

The pictures in this guide show the real app, filled with made-up sample clips. Your screen will show your own recordings. This guide is written for a phone, and everything works the same way on a tablet or computer.

1. Getting started

You can look around without an account

Key Finder, Practice, Jam, the Randomizer, Settings and About all work without signing in. Recording, the Library, Dashboard and Support ask you to sign in with Google when you try to use them.

Signing in

Tap the Sign in with Google button at the top of the screen. The app asks for permission to use Google Drive, and that permission is deliberately narrow: Riff Catalog can only see the files and folders it created itself. Everything else in your Drive stays invisible to it.

Your recordings, song ideas, beats and clip details are saved in a folder called RiffCatalog in your own Google Drive, not on our servers. That’s why they’re yours: you can open that folder in Drive any time.

Installing it like an app

Riff Catalog runs in your web browser, but you can put it on your home screen so it opens like any other app, full screen.

  • iPhone or iPad: open the app in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android: open it in Chrome, tap the three dots, then Install app (or Add to Home screen).
  • Windows or Mac: in Chrome or Edge, click the install icon at the right end of the address bar.

Getting around

  • Menu (three lines): opens the list of sections.
  • Back arrow: goes back to the section you were just in.
  • Red dot: a shortcut straight to Record from anywhere.
  • Refresh button: refreshes the whole app. Use it if something looks stuck or out of date. Recordings waiting on your device are kept.

When a newer version of the app is ready, a banner appears with a Reload button. It never reloads by itself, so it can’t interrupt a recording.

2. Record

Catch an idea the moment it shows up.

  1. Tap the big red button. The first time, your phone asks to use the microphone. Say yes.
  2. Play. The timer counts up and the black box shows that the app is hearing you.
  3. Tap Stop when you’re done.

Metronome & count-in (optional)

Tick this box to get an audible count-in and a visual indicator of your place in the beat. Once recording starts, the audible click will become silent, allowing you to rely on the visual indicator.

  • Tempo (BPM): how fast the click goes. BPM means “beats per minute”. Drag the slider, use the −5 −1 +1 +5 buttons, or tap Tap tempo along with a song to find its speed.
  • Beats per bar: how many clicks before the pattern repeats (4 is the usual). The first click of each group is higher.
  • Count-in: 1 or 2 bars of clicks before recording starts, so you can get ready.
  • The tempo you chose is saved with the clip.

Why does the click stop once recording starts? Your phone’s microphone picks up everything it can hear, including the sound coming out of its own speaker. If the click kept playing, it would be baked into your recording. The app can’t tell whether you’re wearing headphones, and the click is switched off while recording in every case, so it stays silent even with headphones in. The pulsing circles keep time for you instead.

After you stop: the “Recording complete” screen

BPM
The speed of what you played, worked out from the recording (or taken from the metronome if you used it).
Possible keys
The app listens to your recording and guesses which key it’s in. A key is the family of notes a riff is built from, such as “G Major” or “E Minor”. The percentage says how well each key fits. Tap one to pick it. Tap Other… to choose any key yourself, or None if you don’t want a key saved.
The colored note boxes
The seven notes of the selected key. Red is the root (the “home” note), brown is the 3rd, yellow is the 5th, and blue is every other note. You can change these colors in Settings.
Listen before you save
You can play your clip back and drag the bar to skip around. Nothing is saved yet, so you can decide whether it’s a keeper.
Share / Export
Sends the take to another app, or saves it as an audio file, without needing to save it to Drive first. See Share / Export.
Name
Type a name such as “verse riff capo 2”. Providing a name is required.
Save to Drive / Discard
Save to Drive keeps the clip in your Library. Discard throws the take away.

Same name twice? If a clip with that name already exists, the app warns you before saving. You can use a suggested new name, upload anyway (both files are kept, nothing is replaced), or go back and edit the name.

If the internet is slow or unavailable

Recordings are always kept safely on your device first. If saving to Drive takes too long, or you’re offline, the app tells you the clip is saved on your device and will upload by itself.

  • The app retries on its own: after about 20 seconds, then every few minutes (never less often than every 15 minutes), and again whenever you come back online or return to the app.
  • Tap Sync now to try right away.
  • You can play, share, or remove a waiting clip from the orange section in the Library.

Don’t clear your browser’s website data while a clip is waiting. That’s where the waiting recording lives, and clearing it would remove the clip before it reaches Drive.

3. Library

Every clip you’ve saved, with its key, tempo and notes. You need to be signed in to view the Library.

Playing a clip

Tap the blue play button on a clip. Tap it again to pause.

  • Speed (0.5×, 0.75×, 1×): slow a clip down to learn it. The pitch stays the same.
  • 5s back / 5s forward: jump a few seconds to replay a tricky bit.
  • The bar: drag it to move anywhere in the clip.

The buttons on each clip

  • ☆ star: mark a favorite. Tap again to un-star. You can show only starred clips with the filter.
  • ✏️ pencil: rename the clip.
  • 📤 share: send the audio to another app or save it as a file. See Share / Export.
  • ▼ arrow: open the clip’s details (below).

Finding clips

  • Sort (top right): newest first, oldest first, A to Z, by key, or by BPM.
  • Recently Added: tap to open a short list of your five newest clips.
  • Refresh: reloads the list from Drive, for example after saving on another device.
  • Export library (CSV): saves a spreadsheet-style list of your clips with their details (not the audio).

The filter can narrow your clips by key, instrument, tuning, genre, time signature, quality and capo, or show only clips that need lyrics or are starred. Tap mood or tag buttons to show clips that have them. Choices you pick are combined, so the list shrinks as you add more.

A clip’s details

Tap the arrow on a clip to open its details. Fill in as much or as little as you like. Press Save Metadata at the bottom to keep your changes (“metadata” just means “details about the clip”).

Song idea
Attach a song idea from the Randomizer to the clip. Tap Load to pick a saved one, or type your own.
Musical key and Analyze
Choose the key from the list, or tap Analyze to have the app listen to the clip and suggest one. The notes of the chosen key appear underneath in color.
Tempo (BPM) and Tap tempo
Type the speed, or tap Tap tempo in time with the clip.
Time signature, instrument, tuning, genre, capo, quality
Optional labels so you can filter later. A capo is the clamp that raises a guitar’s pitch. Quality is your own rating of the clip.
Needs lyrics
Tick this when a riff still needs words. Those clips show a “needs lyrics” note in the list.
Mood and tags
Tap mood words that fit. For tags, type a word such as “verse” or “bridge” and press Enter. Tap the × on a tag to remove it.
Notes / Lyrics
Anything you like: chords, lyrics, reminders. The app doesn’t limit how much you can type, and you can drag the bottom-right corner of the box to make it taller.

Managing several clips at once

Tick the box on each clip, or Select all. Then:

  • Edit selected: set the same key, tempo, tags and other details on all the ticked clips in one go.
  • Download (arrow down): saves the ticked clips as audio files.
  • Trash can: deletes the ticked clips after asking you to confirm. They go to your Google Drive trash, where you can restore them for 30 days.
  • ×: clears the selection.

4. Share / Export

Get a recording out of Riff Catalog and into anything else: a message to a bandmate, an email, your Files app, a notes app, or another cloud service such as Dropbox or OneDrive.

  • Right after recording: on the Recording complete screen, tap 📤 Share / Export. You don’t have to save to Drive first.
  • Any saved clip: tap the 📤 button on the clip in the Library. This also works for clips still waiting on your device.

On a phone or tablet this opens the device’s own share menu, so you can pick any app on it. On a computer or any device without a share menu, the clip is saved as an audio file in your Downloads folder instead.

iPhone tip: for a clip stored on Drive, the app has to fetch it first. If the share menu doesn’t open the first time, the button changes to Tap to share. Tap it once more and the menu opens.

5. Key Finder

Pick a key and see its notes and chords on a guitar neck or a piano. This works without signing in.

  1. Choose Guitar or Piano at the top.
  2. Pick a key from the list (12 major keys and 12 minor keys).
The seven chords
The row of boxes shows the chords that naturally belong to the key. The number is the chord’s position in the key (1 is home), and the letter shows its type: M major, m minor, dim diminished (the tense, unresolved sound).
Find clips
Under each chord, this button jumps to your Library filtered to clips in that chord’s key.
Relative minor / major
The box with the ring and the ↔ arrow is the relative key. It uses exactly the same seven notes, just starting from a different one, so the two keys sound closely related. Handy when a riff could be read either way.
Tap the notes to play them
Tap any colored note to hear it. Play scale plays all the notes of the key in order.
The guitar neck or piano
Every note of the key is marked, in the same colors as everywhere else in the app. Tap the picture to zoom in. This is useful if you’re jamming and want to see larger versions of these diagrams. Tip: Try using your phone or tablet in portrait AND landscape orientations!

Chord pictures and modes

Below the neck you’ll see a picture of each chord, with a Play button that strums it. How spread out the strum is depends on the Strum speed in Settings. Under the chords, Modes are other scales built from the same seven notes, each starting from a different one. Tap a mode to see its notes.

Reverse Key Lookup

Do you have a riff or lick but don’t know what key it’s in? Tap Reverse Key Lookup, tap the notes you’re playing, then Find Keys. The app lists every key that contains all of them, and how many of your notes match. Clear starts again.

6. Practice

Stop playing the same three things. Practice hands you a random lick (a short phrase) to learn. This works without signing in.

About the practice tabs: the licks, double stops and chord riffs in this section were generated with the help of AI, with a human guiding and checking the results. We’d love your feedback on how to improve them, and on other kinds of practice figures you’d like to see. Send your ideas from Support (the Give feedback tab is perfect for this).

Type
Single-note licks are one note at a time. Double stops are two notes played together. Chord riffs are short chord patterns.
Key
Leave it on Any key to see each lick in its own scale, or pick a key to have the lick moved into it.
Scale
The set of notes the lick is built from, such as Minor Pentatonic or Blues.
Box position
Which part of the neck to play in. A “box” is a standard hand shape for a scale. Choose Free for any position.
Difficulty
Beginner, Intermediate or Advanced, or Any.

Tap 🎲 Random for a surprise, or Browse all to see every lick that matches your choices.

Tab is a simple way to read guitar music: the six lines are the six strings (thinnest on top) and each number is the fret to press. The colors match the rest of the app, so you can see which notes are the root, 3rd and 5th. Tap a lick to zoom in and see it on a picture of the neck.

7. Jam

Drums and a click to play along with. This works without signing in. There are three tabs.

Beats

Scroll down to Choose a beat. There are rock, funk, blues, reggae and waltz beats, fast skate-punk and hardcore beats, thrash and blast-beat metal, and beats in other time signatures such as 3/4, 6/8, 7/8 and 5/4 (each beat shows its time signature and a good range of speeds). Pick one, set the tempo, and tap Play. The beat loops, with a drum fill every fourth bar. Under the tempo, a note tells you which speeds suit that beat. Tap tempo sets the speed from your taps.

Make your own

The grid is one bar split into 16 steps, with a row for each drum. Tap a square to turn that hit on or off, and you hear changes right away while it plays. Type a name and tap Save to keep your beat. It’s kept on this device, and when you’re signed in it’s also saved with your settings in Google Drive so it follows you to your other devices. Load brings a saved beat back. Clear all empties the grid.

Metronome

A simple click. Choose how many beats per bar (the bright light and higher click mark beat 1). Tap along to a song with Tap tempo to find its BPM, then type that into a clip’s details in the Library.

8. Randomizer

Looking for some inspiration to get you started? Our Randomizer wheels will help you develop a song idea as a jumping off point. Successful creative people often talk about how their best work is done when limitations are applied. This is a fun way to push yourself and your music in new directions. You can use this for writing new songs, jamming with friends or just for practice.

Keep in mind that it’s up to you how closely your music adheres to what the wheel lands on. Just because it says “funk” doesn’t mean you have to write a funk song. It might just mean you write a song in YOUR style that has a little bit of funk influence. You get to decide what funk means. It doesn’t have to live up to anyone else’s definition. You can follow it as strictly or loosely as you like. You can also spin again if you get something that doesn’t match your current mood.

The Randomizer works without signing in, though saving ideas needs you to be signed in.

There are nine wheels. Use the drop-down or the arrows on each side of it to change wheels.

Musical Key
Picks the home note your song is built around.
Major / Minor
Picks whether the key sounds bright and happy (major) or darker and more serious (minor).
Style / Genre
Picks a style to lean toward, from Blues and Funk to Metal and Punk.
Theme
Picks something to write about, such as Food, Horror, Cars or Travel.
Mood
Picks the feeling you’re going for, such as Chill, Tense or Uplifting.
Time Signature
Picks how the beats are grouped: usually 4/4, with surprises like 3/4, 6/8 or 7/8.
Chord Progression
Picks chords by their position in the key, from 1 to 7. The results are based on the key you picked earlier: once a key is in your idea, each pick is shown as the actual chord, such as “4 (GMaj)”. Choose your Musical Key (and Major / Minor) first, then spin this wheel as many times as you like to build a progression, which is just a series of chords.
Tempo (BPM)
Picks a speed, from 60 to 200 beats per minute.
Tempo Feel
Picks Normal, Half-Time (feels half as fast) or Double-Time (feels twice as fast) at that tempo.
  • Spin: spin the wheel (or drag it with your finger). It lands on a random pick.
  • Add to idea: adds the pick to Your song idea below. Then move to the next wheel and spin again.
  • Surprise me: spins every wheel for you and fills in the whole idea at once. For the chord progression, it picks a random number of chords, anywhere from 3 to 8, and spins for each one.
  • Undo / Clear: remove the last addition, or empty the idea.

You can also type straight into the idea box. Give it a name and tap Save to Drive. Show saved ideas lists the ones you’ve kept: tap one to load it, or tap Delete to remove it (it goes to your Drive trash, where you can restore it for 30 days). A saved idea can be attached to a clip from the clip’s details in the Library.

9. Dashboard

This page displays your personal stats, regarding usage and features. These are the same stats that get shared with the developer of the app. You need to be signed in to see it, and it only ever shows your own numbers.

It shows how often you’ve opened the app, which days you used it, the sections and features you use most, when you tend to record, and how fast things have been. Further down you can see the feedback and support messages you’ve sent. If the app’s developer has looked at one of your support messages, it may carry a tag such as Bug, Feature request, On hold or Resolved so you can see where it stands. Only the developer can add or change tags. There is also a Download my data button that saves everything the app has kept about you as a file.

The numbers are simple counts. Your recordings, lyrics, clip names and song ideas are never part of them. The full list is in the Privacy Policy.

10. Support

Help, feedback and this guide, all in one place. You need to be signed in. At the very top is the User Guide button, which opens this page. Below it are three tabs.

Please be patient: Riff Catalog is developed by a single person, so it may take some time to get a response to a support request. Every message is read.

Contact support

Describe what’s wrong or what you’d like to know, then tap Send to support. We reply by email.

  • Add a screenshot: attach up to 3 pictures. They’re shrunk before sending and only the app’s owner can see them. Please don’t include anything private.
  • Include technical details: adds your app version, device, system, browser and screen size, plus short codes for recent problems. It never includes your recordings, clip names, lyrics or anything you’ve written in the app. Leaving this ticked helps us find problems faster.

Give feedback

Rate the app from half a star to five (tap the left half of a star for a half), then say what’s useful, what’s frustrating, and what you’d love it to do. All the text boxes are optional. Feedback is read but not answered one by one, so use Contact support if you need a reply.

Debug

For when something isn’t working. It shows a log of what the app has been doing, and a Send report button that sends a short technical report (no recordings or clip names). You can also save the log to your Drive or copy it. You’ll rarely need this unless support asks.

11. Settings

Interval colors
Choose the colors used for the root, 3rd, 5th and other notes. They’re used on every guitar neck, keyboard, chord picture, tab and note row in the app. Pick a color from the swatch or type a color code. Reset to defaults puts them back.
Note names
Show the black keys as sharps (C♯) or flats (D♭) everywhere. Your saved clips aren’t changed.
Strum speed
How spread out the notes of a chord are when you tap Play on a chord in Key Finder. Slower makes each note easier to hear. Hear it plays a sample.
Appearance
Match your device, or force Light or Dark. This is saved on this device only, so your phone and computer can look different.
Microphone and speaker
Choose which microphone Riff Catalog records with, for example a USB audio interface, and which speaker or headphones it plays through. If you don’t see the names of your gear, tap Show my devices and allow the microphone once. Test microphone shows a level bar that moves when you play, so you can check your interface works and isn’t too loud. Play a test sound checks your speaker. Some browsers don’t let apps choose the speaker; if yours doesn’t, the page says so and Riff Catalog plays through whatever the device is set to use. This is saved on this device only.
Account & storage
Shows who’s signed in and has the Sign out button. It also tells you whether anything is waiting on this device to upload. Saved clip details are kept on the device so the Library opens quickly; Clear forgets them (they download again next time). Your recordings are never deleted by this.

When you’re signed in, your colors, note names and strum speed are saved to your Google Drive and follow you to your other devices. Appearance and your microphone and speaker choice stay on each device.

13. Working offline

Once you’ve opened the installed app online, it can open with no connection. Offline you can still record. The clip is stored on your device and uploads by itself when you’re back online (see Record). Key Finder, Practice, Jam and the Randomizer wheel keep working. Anything that reads from or saves to Google Drive, such as the Library, needs a connection.

14. If something goes wrong

The Library is empty or out of date
Tap Refresh in the Library. Check that the top of the screen says Drive with a green tick. If it shows a clock, tap the orange Drive disconnected bar to reconnect.
Saving says “Checking…” for a long time
It gives up after a short wait, keeps your clip safely on your device and uploads it later by itself. You don’t need to do anything. You can tap Sync now to try again.
The screen looks stuck or out of date
Tap the Refresh button at the top right. Waiting recordings are kept.
The microphone doesn’t work
Check that your browser or phone has permission to use the microphone for Riff Catalog, and that no other app is using it.
Still stuck?
Go to Support and send a message, with a screenshot if you can. We reply by email.

15. Glossary of music terms

BPM (tempo)
Beats per minute: how fast a song feels. 60 is slow, 120 is a brisk walk, 160 is fast.
Key
The family of seven notes a riff is built from, named for its home note: G Major, E Minor and so on.
Major / Minor
Two flavors of key. Major sounds bright and happy. Minor sounds darker and more serious.
Root, 3rd, 5th
The home note of a key, and the notes two and four steps up from it. Together they make the main chord of the key, so they’re colored differently.
Relative key
The major and minor keys that share the same seven notes (G Major and E Minor, for example).
Chord
Several notes played together.
Scale
A set of notes played in order, such as Minor Pentatonic (five notes, the classic rock and blues sound).
Lick
A short musical phrase you can learn and reuse.
Tab
A way of writing guitar music using numbers on six lines for the six strings.
Capo
A clamp across the neck that raises every string’s pitch.
Tuning
What pitch the strings are set to. Standard tuning is the usual one.
Time signature
How beats are grouped. 4/4 is the most common; 3/4 is a waltz.
Metadata
Details about a clip, such as its key, tempo, tags and notes.
